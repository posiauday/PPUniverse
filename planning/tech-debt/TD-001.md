## What was deferred and why
`.github/workflows/ci.yml`'s "Dependency audit" step (`pnpm audit --audit-level=high`) runs with `continue-on-error: true`. At MVP-001 there was no real dependency tree to triage against yet, and making it blocking with only three scaffolded packages risked a noisy false-positive gate before any real risk existed. `docs/03-trd.md`'s deployment section lists "dependency scan" as a required PR check but does not specify blocking behavior, so this was a scoping call, not a requirement violation.

## Risk if left unresolved
As `apps/web`, `apps/worker`, and future domain/adapter packages pull in real dependencies (payments, storage, identity SDKs), a known-vulnerable transitive dependency could merge silently, since the audit step currently cannot fail the build.

## Proposed resolution
Once MVP-002 or later stories introduce a meaningful dependency surface, flip the step to blocking (remove `continue-on-error: true`) and establish an exception process (e.g., a documented allowlist file) for accepted/unfixable advisories, per NFR guidance on supply-chain scanning in `docs/08-security-privacy-compliance.md`.

## Resolved by (story/commit, once closed)
MVP-002. `pnpm audit --audit-level=high` found real HIGH-severity findings (see TD-002), which were triaged and fixed. With the audit now clean, `.github/workflows/ci.yml`'s dependency-audit step was made blocking (`continue-on-error` removed).
